No Tax on Tips Calculator

This no-tax-on-tips calculator shows how much of your tips you can deduct from federal taxable income — up to $25,000 a year for tax years 2025–2028 — and the tax that saves you. Enter your W-2 and any self-employed tips; it applies the cap and the income phase-out and estimates your deduction instantly.

Updated 16 September 2026

Who can deduct tips (tipped occupations list)

The deduction is for people in jobs that customarily and regularly receive tips — servers, bartenders, baristas, hairstylists and barbers, nail technicians, delivery drivers, valets, hotel housekeepers and similar roles. The IRS publishes the qualifying occupation list, and only tips earned in one of those roles count.

You also need a valid Social Security number, and married taxpayers must file jointly to claim it. If your job isn't on the list — or the money is a service charge the employer sets rather than a voluntary tip — it doesn't qualify. Check the current list in the IRS guidance before you rely on the deduction.

Worked example: server with $12,000 in tips

A restaurant server reports $12,000 of tips on their W-2 and earns $45,000 in total.

  • Qualified tips = $12,000 — well under the $25,000 cap.
  • Income is below $150,000, so there's no phase-out reduction.
  • Deduction = $12,000. In the 12% bracket, that saves about $1,440 in federal income tax.

Enter your own reported tips above to see your number. If your tips exceed $25,000, the deduction stops at the cap; if your income is high, the phase-out reduces it.

W-2 tips vs self-employed tips

If you're an employee, your qualifying tips are the amounts reported on your W-2. If you're self-employed or a gig worker (reported on a 1099), tips can also qualify — but only up to the net profit of the business they came from. You can't deduct more in tips than that work actually earned.

The calculator has separate fields for W-2 tips and self-employed tips, and it caps the self-employed portion at the net profit you enter. Combined, they're then limited to the $25,000 annual cap. Keep your books clean so the two figures reconcile with what's reported to the IRS.

Income phase-out

Above a modified adjusted gross income of $150,000 (single) or $300,000 (joint), the tips deduction phases out — it's reduced as income rises and eventually reaches zero. Most tipped workers earn below these thresholds, so they get the full deduction up to the cap.

The calculator applies the reduction automatically. The exact per-dollar phase-out rate comes from the IRS Schedule 1-A instructions, which we're confirming against that source — so treat results near the thresholds as an estimate until you verify your own figures.

Records to keep

Keep a daily tip record and your pay stubs, and make sure your reported tips match your W-2 (box for allocated/reported tips) or your 1099 income. Only voluntary tips count — mandatory service charges the employer adds to a bill are treated as regular wages, not tips.

Good records matter if the IRS asks you to substantiate the deduction, and they help you enter accurate numbers here. When you file, you'll claim the deduction on Schedule 1-A whether you itemize or take the standard deduction. Confirm the current rules on IRS.gov.

Frequently asked questions

How will the no tax on tips work?

Qualifying tips become deductible from your federal taxable income, up to $25,000 a year for 2025–2028, claimed on Schedule 1-A. You still report your tips as normal; the deduction then lowers the income tax you owe on them. It doesn't remove Social Security or Medicare, and it phases out at higher incomes.

What's the downside to no tax on tips?

The main limits: it only reduces federal income tax (not FICA or most state tax), it's capped at $25,000, it phases out above $150,000/$300,000, and it only covers voluntary tips in listed occupations. It's also temporary — tax years 2025 through 2028 — unless extended.

Has no tax on tips kicked in yet?

Yes — it applies from tax year 2025, so tips earned in 2025 can be claimed on the return you file in early 2026. It's a deduction taken at filing time rather than an immediate change to your paycheck withholding, though the IRS may adjust withholding guidance separately.

Which states are allowing no tax on tips?

This is a federal deduction, so it reduces your federal income tax regardless of state. Whether your state also exempts tips depends on that state's own law — many still tax tips in full. Check your state revenue department; this calculator estimates the federal deduction only.

How do I calculate my no tax on tips?

Add up your qualifying tips (W-2 tips, plus self-employed tips limited to that business's net profit), cap the total at $25,000, reduce it if your income is above the threshold, and multiply by your tax bracket to estimate the saving. The calculator above does each step for you.

How much will no tax on tips save me?

Your saving is your deductible tips times your federal tax bracket. For example, $12,000 of tips in the 12% bracket saves about $1,440; $25,000 (the cap) in the 22% bracket saves about $5,500. Enter your figures above for a personal estimate.

How does the new no tax on tips work?

It's an above-the-line federal deduction for voluntary tips in tipped occupations, up to $25,000 for 2025–2028, available whether you itemize or not. You need a valid SSN, and married filers must file jointly. It reduces federal income tax only and phases out at higher incomes.

Does everyone get a $3,000 tax refund?

No — there's no flat refund. Your benefit depends entirely on how much you earn in qualifying tips and your tax bracket. Some workers save a few hundred dollars, others more, and high earners may be phased out. Use the calculator with your own numbers rather than relying on a headline figure.
