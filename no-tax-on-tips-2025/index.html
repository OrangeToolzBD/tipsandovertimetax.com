No Tax on Tips for 2025 Returns

The tips deduction applies from tax year 2025, so tips you earned in 2025 can be deducted on the federal return you file in early 2026 — up to $25,000 of qualifying, reported tips. You claim it on Schedule 1-A whether you itemize or not, subject to the income phase-out above $150,000 (single) or $300,000 (joint).

What changed for 2025

2025 is the first tax year the tips deduction is available. Under the 2025 law, workers in tipped occupations can deduct up to $25,000 of voluntary, reported tips from federal taxable income. It's an above-the-line deduction — you don't have to itemize — and it reduces income tax only, not Social Security or Medicare.

The core rules that apply for 2025 are the $25,000 cap, the income phase-out starting at $150,000 (single) / $300,000 (joint), a valid Social Security number, and joint filing for married couples. Confirm the specifics in the IRS guidance before filing.

How 2025 tips were reported

Because the law arrived during the year, reporting for 2025 may rely on the tips already shown on your W-2 (for employees) or in your business income (for self-employed workers), with IRS transition guidance filling gaps. Your employer reports the tips you declared through the year, and that figure supports your deduction.

Keep your final pay stub, your W-2, and any personal daily tip log. If your reported tips look low because some weren't declared, you generally can't retroactively deduct unreported income. Match your calculator entry to what's actually reported to the IRS, and check the current-year instructions on IRS.gov.

Already filed? Amending a 2025 return

If you filed your 2025 return before claiming the deduction — or filed early software that didn't include it — you can generally fix it by filing an amended return (Form 1040-X) to add the Schedule 1-A deduction. This can increase your refund or reduce tax owed.

There are time limits for amending (typically three years from the original filing), so don't wait too long. Gather your W-2 and tip records first, run the calculator to estimate the deduction, and consider a tax professional if your situation is complex. Confirm amendment steps on IRS.gov.

Use the calculator

Frequently asked questions

How does the No Tax on Tips work?

Qualifying voluntary, reported tips are deductible from federal taxable income, up to $25,000 for 2025, claimed on Schedule 1-A. You still report tips as normal; the deduction lowers the income tax on them. It needs a valid SSN, joint filing for married couples, and phases out at higher incomes.

Are servers still getting taxed on tips?

Tips are still reported and still subject to Social Security and Medicare, and often state tax. What changed is that qualifying tips can now be deducted from federal income tax, up to $25,000 — so servers below the income thresholds owe less federal income tax on their tips than before.

How is the No Tax on Tips going to work exactly now?

For 2025 you report tips through the year as usual, then claim the deduction on Schedule 1-A when you file in early 2026. If you already filed without it, you can amend. The cap, phase-out and occupation rules all apply; use the calculator to estimate your figure.

Who gets the new $6000 tax break?

The $6,000 figure is a different provision — an additional deduction for taxpayers aged 65 and older — not the tips deduction, which is capped at $25,000. If you're a senior, that separate senior deduction may also apply; the tips deduction here is for reported tips in tipped occupations.
