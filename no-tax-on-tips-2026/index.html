No Tax on Tips in 2026

For tax year 2026, the tips deduction continues: workers in tipped occupations can deduct up to $25,000 of voluntary, reported tips from federal taxable income, phasing out above $150,000 (single) or $300,000 (joint). You'll claim 2026 tips on the return you file in early 2027, on Schedule 1-A, whether or not you itemize.

2026 limits

The 2026 rules mirror 2025: a $25,000 annual cap on qualifying tips, an income phase-out beginning at $150,000 (single) and $300,000 (joint) of modified adjusted gross income, a valid Social Security number requirement, and joint filing for married couples. The deduction reduces federal income tax only.

Because 2026 is a full year under the law from day one, reporting should be cleaner than the 2025 transition year. The cap and thresholds are fixed amounts rather than inflation-indexed in the guidance issued so far — always confirm the current-year figures in the IRS guidance before you file.

W-2 and 1099 reporting

For 2026, employees will see their reported tips on their W-2, and employers are expected to identify qualifying tips more consistently now that the rule has been in place a year. Self-employed and gig workers report tips as part of business income, deductible only up to that business's net profit.

Keep a daily tip record through 2026 so your reported figure is accurate and reconciles with your W-2 or 1099. Only voluntary tips qualify — automatic service charges are wages, not tips. Follow the current Schedule 1-A instructions when you prepare your return.

Filing in 2027

You claim the 2026 deduction when you file your 2026 federal return in early 2027. It's taken on Schedule 1-A, above the line, so you can use it alongside the standard deduction. As with 2025, withholding during 2026 may not reflect the deduction, so the benefit usually appears as a lower balance due or a larger refund at filing.

Plan ahead: estimate your 2026 deduction now, keep your records through the year, and revisit the figure if your income approaches the phase-out. Confirm the filing steps and any updated forms on IRS.gov closer to filing season.

Frequently asked questions

How will no tax on tips work?

In 2026, qualifying voluntary, reported tips are deductible from federal taxable income up to $25,000, claimed on Schedule 1-A when you file in 2027. You still report tips as normal; the deduction lowers the income tax on them. It needs a valid SSN, joint filing for married couples, and phases out at higher incomes.

Is no tax on tips passed yet?

Yes — it was enacted in the 2025 law and applies to tax years 2025 through 2028, so it's in effect for 2026. You claim 2026 tips on the return filed in early 2027. Always confirm the current rules and any updates on IRS.gov before relying on them.

Who gets the new $6000 tax break?

The $6,000 deduction is a separate provision for taxpayers aged 65 and older, not the tips deduction (capped at $25,000). A senior who also earns tips could potentially benefit from both, each under its own rules. This page covers the tips deduction specifically.

Which states are allowing no tax on tips?

The tips deduction is federal, so it reduces federal income tax in every state. Whether your state also exempts tips depends on that state's own tax law — many still tax tips fully. Check your state revenue department; the calculator here estimates the federal deduction only.
